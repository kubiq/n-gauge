02 Before wiring — two checks

Two minutes with a multimeter now saves a confusing first session in the car.

Check 1 · Remove the 120 Ω terminator

The car's CAN bus is already terminated; an extra 120 Ω can corrupt it. Measure resistance across CANH ↔ CANL on the transceiver board alone:

  • ~120 Ω → it has a terminator (marked 121) → desolder that resistor.
  • Open / very high → no terminator → leave it as is.

Both board variants work: AliExpress = header pins + CTX/CRX labels; Waveshare/Botland = screw terminal + CAN TX/CAN RX labels. Same SN65HVD230 chip, same wiring.

Check 2 · Find OBD pins 6 & 14 — don't trust wire colors

Cheap pigtails use random colors. In continuity / beep mode, touch one probe to a flying wire and the other to each metal pin in the OBD plug. Pin numbers are molded in: 1–8 top row, 9–16 bottom. Label the CAN-H and CAN-L wires once found.

Bus speed 500 kbit/s · no extra terminator on our side · power the ESP32 from the power bank during testing.

ESP32 GPIO4 (CAN RX) ──►  SN65HVD230 CRX
ESP32 3.3V           ──►  SN65HVD230 3V3
ESP32 GND            ──►  SN65HVD230 GND
SN65HVD230 CANH      ──►  OBD-II pin 6   (CAN-H)
SN65HVD230 CANL      ──►  OBD-II pin 14  (CAN-L)
ESP32 GND            ──►  OBD-II pin 4 or 5  (GND)

04 Flashing the sniffer

Sketch: firmware/can_sniffer/can_sniffer.ino

  1. Arduino IDE with the ESP32 board package (Boards Manager → "esp32" by Espressif).
  2. Tools → Board → ESP32 Dev Module (or your specific board).
  3. Select the correct Port.
  4. Open the sketch → Upload.
  5. Open Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, line ending = Newline.

If GPIO4/5 clash with your board, change CAN_TX_GPIO / CAN_RX_GPIO at the top of the sketch. Avoid strapping pins 0 / 2 / 12 / 15 and input-only 34–39.

05 Test session in the car

  1. Plug the pigtail into the OBD-II port (under the dash, driver side).
  2. Power the ESP32 from your laptop's USB (the same cable shows the Serial Monitor).
  3. Ignition ON (engine off is fine for sniffing; run it for real oil/boost values).
  4. Open Serial Monitor.

What you should see

A stream of [RX] xxx [8] .. lines = CAN frames. ✓ This confirms your tap works. If nothing streams, jump to Troubleshooting.

Built-in commands

Type a command in the Serial Monitor and press Enter:

CommandDoes
sToggle the live frame dump on/off (turn OFF to read replies clearly)
oilRequest standard oil temp (Mode 01 PID 5C)
boostRequest manifold pressure (Mode 01 PID 0B)
01 0CRequest RPM — sanity-check the request path
22 XXXXEnhanced request, 2-byte DID (the real i30 N oil-temp DID)
helpReprint commands

If oil returns a sensible temperature — great, standard PID works. If it returns a NEGATIVE response, the i30 N doesn't expose 015C → use the enhanced Mode 22 DID below.

06 The real oil temp — enhanced Mode 22

The i30 N's true oil temp (and true boost) live in enhanced PIDs requested with Mode 22 to ECU header 7E0. Don't guess the DID — use a community-validated list.

  • Car Scanner ELM OBD2 app → the "Hyundai i30 N" custom-PID profile.
  • Torque Pro extended-PID CSVs shared on i30 N forums / Reddit.

Each entry gives the request bytes (e.g. 22 XXXX) and a scaling formula. In the Serial Monitor, send the DID; the sketch prints the raw response bytes. Apply the formula — commonly A − 40 or (A·256 + B)/scale — to get °C.

07 Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause / fix
No [RX] frames at allCAN-H / CAN-L swapped → swap pins 6/14. Or ignition off. Or GND not connected.
twai_start failed on bootWrong TX/RX GPIOs for your board, or pins already in use. Change them in the sketch.
Frames garbled / error burstsWrong bus speed (must be 500 kbit/s) or the 120 Ω terminator wasn't removed.
NEGATIVE (7F) responsesThat PID/DID isn't supported → use the enhanced Mode 22 DID from the list.
A few frames, then silenceBus went to sleep → turn ignition on / wake the car.

08 Power plan — Option B chosen

Powered from OBD-II pin 16 (+12 V) via a 12 V→5 V buck — the whole gauge stays a single OBD-port plug, no fuse-box wiring.

Pin 16 is constant battery power, so the firmware manages sleep to avoid parked drain:

  • CAN bus watched → no frames for ~30 s → AMOLED off + esp_deep_sleep.
  • Ignition on wakes the bus → ESP32 wakes and resumes the gauge.
  • The i30 N diagnostic CAN bus sleeps after ignition-off, so this triggers cleanly.
⚠ Open TODO · measure quiescent draw

Once sleep is implemented, measure actual parked current on the 12 V feed — including the buck's own quiescent draw (cheap bucks pull several mA at no load). Target a few mA or less; if too high, use a low-Iq buck or a hard cutoff. Fallback: Option A — switched 12 V from a cabin fuse, which cuts power fully with the car.

09 After the prototype succeeds

  1. Confirm oil temp + boost read correctly and update fast enough.
  2. Order the Waveshare ESP32-S3 1.75" AMOLED Round Touch (466×466, ~$50).
  3. Move CAN to the S3 header: GPIO17 = CAN TX, GPIO16 = CAN RX (GPIO18 free).
  4. Build the LVGL gauge UI — oil-temp + boost, touch to switch, brightness.
  5. Add sleep-on-bus-idle power management, then measure quiescent draw.
  6. 3D-print the vent panel in PETG / ASA (not PLA — dash heat), keeping OEM louvers working.
  7. Final power: OBD pin 16 → 12 V→5 V buck → display 5 V pin.

10 Mounting — vent cover + display pod

Location: the driver-side fixed side-window demister grille on the dash top by the A-pillar (part 97383-G3000-TRY). It blows up/out at the glass, so a pod mounted in front, angled inboard at the driver, sits out of the airflow path. Full demist kept, no trade-off.

  1. Pop off the OEM demister grille (clips) and study its mounting + the grille opening.
  2. Measure with calipers or 3D-scan the opening and the dash-top curvature.
  3. Model a replacement cover that recreates the fixed grille slots (or reuses the OEM grille insert) — demist airflow to the side window untouched.
  4. Add a low-profile display pod on the driver/inboard side, in front of the grille, angled at the eyes, with a recessed circular pocket + lip/gasket for the round AMOLED. Keep it clear of the outboard airflow.
  5. Route the display FPC + wiring down behind the A-pillar trim / into the dash to the ESP32 and power.
  6. Print in ASA / PETG (dash heat + UV); matte finish or wrap to match the dash.
⚠ Near the windscreen — keep it low

This spot is at the windscreen base: too tall blocks forward view and reflects in the glass. Keep the pod low, angle the face slightly down, and use auto-dim (day/night) + an anti-glare film to kill windscreen reflection.

OEM part number (confirmed — PD / Elantra GT catalog)

Target = the fixed side-window demister grille: Nozzle Assy-Side Defroster, LH = 97383-G3000-TRY (driver side). RH = 97393-G3000-TRY.

  • Single variant (-TRY, black) — no colour-suffix ambiguity.
  • Order one as a donor to 3D-scan; keep the fitted one intact.
  • Not this build: the adjustable louvered corner vent lower down = 97480-G3000-[color].

Custom pod — fully bespoke

No usable OEM-vent STL exists, and off-the-shelf holders are RHD-only and sized for 52/60 mm gauges. So this is modeled from scratch, exactly for our ~46 mm display — which is the point: it's our part. (See References for a RHD holder worth eyeballing for the approach.)

  1. Remove the OEM demister grille; note how it clips in and the grille opening pattern.
  2. Capture geometry — phone 3D-scan of the removed grille + opening; fallback calipers + photos with a scale marker.
  3. Design a two-part piece: a cover that recreates the fixed grille slots (keeps demist airflow), and a pod holding the ~46 mm AMOLED at driver angle with a bezel lip and rear cable exit.
  4. Print test rings of just the opening profile first to dial in fit before the full part.
  5. Final print in ASA / PETG, matte or wrapped to match the dash.

3D scanning (phone → cloud)

Using the RealityScan phone app (Epic cloud) — the Linux desktop build needs an NVIDIA ≥8 GB GPU we don't have, so cloud is the path. Photos, not video — ~40–60 stills, 70–80 % overlap, 3 heights all around, tap-to-focus.

⚠ Monotone black = blobby mesh → add features

Stipple the show face with a white paint marker (POSCA): many small ~1–2 mm dots, RANDOM spacing (never a grid), densely all over. White-on-black is ideal contrast; a grid confuses the matcher.

It's your car's part, so use a water-based paint pen → wipes off with isopropyl alcohol (test an edge first). Keep dots off the mounting/clip faces. Use soft diffuse light to avoid gloss hotspots. Avoid uniform white "scanning spray" — that's for laser scanners, not photogrammetry.

  • Alignment/scale: textured background + a couple of printed coded targets; set scale from two dots a known distance apart.
  • Flip the part to capture the mounting/clip side.
  • Hybrid — don't scan the slots: caliper the grille slot width/spacing/count + mounting dims and model them parametrically; use the scan only for the curved shell. Export OBJ (meshes ~0.2–0.5 mm noisy).

CAD on Linux

Fusion 360 isn't native on Linux → model in FreeCAD (parametric, native — pod + snap-fit, mesh as reference) or Blender (mesh cleanup / organic surfaces).

11 Feature roadmap

What the gauge grows into. Oil temp, cold-engine warning, boost and the over-temp guard are the v1 core; connectivity and the GPS track come much later.

Hardware note · GPS — buy the Standard version

The GPS variant is out of stock, and you don't need it. Add a GNSS module later (see order list — NEO-M9N for track), read-only into GPIO18 over UART. It sits in the tube by the vent opening near the windscreen → good sky view. CAN stays on GPIO16/17; buttons are onboard. Nothing about v1 changes.

  • Buttons to switch screens without touching the display

12 Safety notes

  • Reading CAN is passive/low-risk — but never leave anything transmitting garbage while driving.
  • Don't power the display from OBD pin 16 (constant 12 V = battery drain) — use switched 12 V.
  • Be careful pulling dash trim near the airbag; disconnect the battery for deeper work.
  • Keep the screen glanceable and dimmable — driver-distraction rules.
